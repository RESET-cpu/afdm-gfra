Learning Sparsity Pattern: An Adaptive Joint Detection for AFDM
Grant-Free System in LEO Satellites Communication
Chao Ding, Lixia Xiao, Jiaxi Zhou, Lidong Zhu, Pei Xiao, Senior Member, IEEE, and Tao Jiang, Fellow, IEEE

Abstract—In this paper, an adaptive sparse pattern learning
aided joint activity detection and channel estimation scheme is
proposed for grant-free random access with affine frequency
division multiplexing (AFDM) in LEO satellite communication
systems. Specifically, the channel parameters and active index
will be detected in the context of unknown sparsity. First, a
sparse prior is preset to perform active detection and channel
estimation. Subsequently, the prior is refined by a graph network architecture, where the delay-Doppler grid is modeled as
the graph nodes delivering the message. Furthermore, a novel
graph aggregation module based on self-attention mechanism is
designed to capture the correlation between nodes to infer the unknown sparsity patterns inherent in dynamic terrestrial-satellite
links (TSL). Simulation results demonstrate that, compared with
conventional nearest neighbor sparsity pattern learning (NNSPL),
the proposed scheme achieves a 2 dB improvement in normalized
mean-square error (NMSE) and reduces pilot overhead by
approximately 25%.
Index Terms—Affine frequency division multiplexing (AFDM),
grant-free random access (GF-RA), joint active detection and
channel estimation (JADCE), graph neural network (GNN).

I. I NTRODUCTION

OW earth orbit (LEO) satellites communication, enabling
global coverage and ubiquitous connectivity, have been
envisaged as a fundamental component of future Internet of
Things (IoT) [1]. Over the next decade, large-scale constellations comprising thousands of LEO satellites will be deployed
to serve billions of IoT devices [2]. However, owing to massive
device connectivity and long terrestrial-satellite link (TSL)
distances, conventional grant-based access mechanism gives
rise to prohibitive transmission latency [3]. Accordingly, grantfree random access (GF-RA), which significantly reduces
signaling overhead and access latency by streamlining the
access process, constitutes as a promising alternative for LEO
satellite IoT.
In GF-RA systems, each user transmits data directly to
LEO satellites without prior scheduling. Nevertheless, the
high Doppler shift caused by the mobility of LEO satellites
poses severe interference for data transmission. To address this

challenge, orthogonal time frequency space (OTFS) modulation is incorporated into GF-RA systems [4]. However, the
high complexity of the 2D transformation in OTFS limits
its practical deployment. In contrast, affine frequency division multiplexing (AFDM), based on 1D chirp modulation,
is capable of achieving comparable performance with lower
complexity [5]. Owing to these attractive features, AFDM
emerges as a promising candidate for low-complexity and
reliable GF-RA systems.
To unleash the full potential of AFDM-GF-RA, accurate
joint activity detection and channel estimation (JADCE) is a
critical prerequisite. By exploiting the sporadic sparsity of IoT
device, JADCE has been widely formulated as a compressed
sensing (CS) problem [6]–[12]. Specifically, representative
algorithms including approximate message passing (AMP)
[6], orthogonal matching pursui (OMP) [7], sparse Bayesian
learning (SBL) [8], have attracted considerable attention in
terrestrial networks owing to their efficient implementation.
However, such approaches fail to exploit the block sparsity
induced by the large delay and high Doppler characteristics of
TSLs. As a remedy, advanced schemes have been developed
to facilitate block sparse pattern through structured prior
modeling or clustering methods, such as pattern coupled SBL
[9], sparsity pattern learning (SPL) [10], [11], and modified
variance state propagation [12]. However, since their convergence performance depends on predefined hyperparameters,
the applicability of these schemes remains constrained, making
it difficult to fully exploit the evolving structural sparsity in
TSLs.
Benefiting from powerful feature extraction capabilities,
deep learning-based schemes have been investigated for
JADCE to alleviate the dependence on empirical models
[13]–[15]. Representative works include data-driven [13]
and model-driven methods [14], [15]. However, data-driven
schemes suffer from limited interpretability, while modeldriven approaches exhibit limited gains due to restricted parameterization. Moreover, owing to the absence of explicit
modeling for complex sparsity patterns, these architectures are
unable to exploit structural correlations, resulting in degraded
performance in AFDM-GF-RA systems.
Against the above background, the contributions of this
paper are summarized as follows:
1) We propose a graph neural network-based adaptive sparsity pattern learning (GNN-ASPL) scheme for AFDMGF-RA system. Specifically, it extends the conventional
sparse clustering methods into a modified message passing paradigm, and employs GNN to infer the unknown
sparse pattern in an adaptive manner.

corresponding to the T layers of the network. In the t-th
stage, the GNN module from layer t − 1 is first trained to
refine the sparsity prior, with parameters of other layers kept
fixed. Subsequently, the t-th AMP module is optimized based

A. Simulation Setup
This section considers a typical LEO-enabled GF-RA system, where a satellite serves K = 100 potential devices within
its coverage area. Each device is activated with a probability of
pk = 0.1 and transmits data using the AFDM waveform with
a carrier frequency of 4 GHz and a subcarrier spacing of 15
kHz. The non-terrestrial channel model recommended in [16]
is adopted, with the number of paths P = 3. Due to coarse precompensation, the residual delay and Doppler shifts for each
device are assumed within a small range, where the maximum
normalization delay τmax is set to 2 and the maximum residual
Doppler is set to 30 kHz. The delays and Doppler are assumed
to be aligned with the grid points, with (Nl , Nk ) = (2, 4).
In the offline training stage, GNN-ASPL is implemented
via Pytorch platform on Dell T640 with a NVIDIA RTX
3090 GPU. To achieve satisfactory performance, over 10,000
pairs of training data (Y, Ψ, Z, σ 2 ) are randomly generated at
different SNRs and a ten-layer GNN-ASPL is trained for each
SNR. The Adam optimizer is adopted, with an initial learning
rate 5 × 10−3 and a batchsize of 128.

V. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, an AFDM-GF-RA system was investigated for
LEO satellite IoT to mitigate excessive signaling overhead and
access latency. To enable reliable uplink access, the JADCE
problem was first formulated as a structured compressed
sensing task. To address this problem, a GNN-ASPL algorithm was proposed. Specifically, a self-attention-based graph
aggregation module was developed to refine sparsity patterns,
which was further embedded into a deep unfolding framework
to enhance sparse recovery. Simulation results verified the
significant performance advantages of the proposed approach
and demonstrated its strong generalization capabilities under
dynamic TSL conditions.
