Learned Score-Based Turbo Message Passing for Channel Recovery
From Spatially Compressed Measurements
Yiming Wei,Student Member , IEEEand Xiaojun Yuan,Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—Hybrid beamforming (HBF) reduces the hardware
cost of massive MIMO base stations, but its channel acquisition
requires recovering the full spatial-domain channel from highly
compressed beam-domain observations. This paper proposes a
learned score-based turbo message passing (L-STMP) framework
for uplink HBF channel recovery under extreme spatial compres-
sion. By deeply unfolding the turbo message-passing loop and
introducing trainable calibration parameters and learnable score
priors in each layer, L-STMP liberates STMP from the idealized
compression-matrix requirements of right-rotational invariance,
and reduces inference complexity to only several unfolded layers.
In addition, to preserve dominant channel components that
random discrete Fourier transform (DFT) selection may miss,
we introduce precoding matrix indicator (PMI)-assisted beam
selection as a low-overhead directional prior . Simulation results
demonstrate that L-STMP consistently outperforms existing
baselines and obtains substantial reconstruction gains under
PMI-assisted compression in channel reconstruction.
Index Terms—Hybrid beamforming, massive MIMO, channel
recovery, PMI, learned score-based turbo message passing.
I. INTRODUCTION
M
ASSIVE multiple-antenna technology is a key enabler
for improving spectral efficiency, coverage, and spatial
multiplexing gains in 5G-Advanced and future 6G systems
[1]. However, as the number of base-station antennas in-
creases, fully digital architectures that assign an independent
radio-frequency (RF) chain, analog-to-digital/digital-to-analog
converter (ADC/DAC), and mixer to each antenna become
prohibitive in hardware cost, power consumption, and cir-
cuit complexity [1], [2]. Hybrid analog-digital beamforming
(HBF) alleviates this burden by using far fewer RF chains
than antennas, especially in millimeter-wave, terahertz, and
extremely large-scale array systems [2], [3]. This hardware-
efficient design, however, forces the base station to observe
uplink pilots through a low-dimensional analog or beam-
domain projection. Recovering the full spatial-domain channel
from such compressed observations is therefore a highly
underdetermined inverse problem, whose accuracy directly
affects digital precoding, scheduling, interference suppression,
and link adaptation.
Existing HBF channel recovery methods mainly exploit
angular or beam-domain sparsity and formulate the problem
as compressed sensing [2]–[4]. Representative approaches
include orthogonal matching pursuit/distributed compressive
sensing (OMP/DCS)-based support detection [5], [6], approx-
imate message passing/learned approximate message passing
(AMP/LAMP)-based message passing [7], [8], and sparse

Bayesian learning (SBL)-based Bayesian recovery [9]. How-
ever, under extreme spatial compression where only a small
subset of beams is retained, these methods degrade severely:
grid-based sparse models suffer from power leakage and
beam shift [4], [10], dominant-path supports may be missed
by aggressive beam-domain subsampling [3], [11], and even
learned priors remain tied to channel sparsity assumptions that
inadequately capture real-world channel distributions [10]–
[12]. Score-based generative models provide a more expres-
sive prior for high-dimensional channel recovery by learning
the gradient of the log channel distribution [13]. However,
diffusion-style posterior sampling typically requires hundreds
of reverse-time iterations [14]. Score-based turbo message
passing (STMP) offers a low-latency alternative [15], [16], but
its denoising module requires the input to follow an additive
white Gaussian noise (AWGN) model, which holds only
when the compression matrix is right-rotationally invariant
and the system dimension goes to infinity. In practical HBF
systems, however, the finite-dimensional compression matrices
— especially structured beam-selection designs — can hardly
meet these conditions, causing the recovery performance to
degrade severely.
The above limitation motivates the learned STMP (L-
STMP) receiver proposed in this paper. Firstly, we deeply un-
fold the turbo message-passing loop into a multi-layer network
and introduce trainable calibration parameters and learnable
score priors in each layer. This design liberates STMP from the
idealized requirements of infinite system dimension and right-
rotationally invariant compression matrix, thereby leaving
room for flexible analog beamforming design. We show that L-
STMP reduces the inference complexity from upto hundreds of
iterations of STMP to only several unfolded layers. Secondly,
although random discrete Fourier transform (DFT) beam selec-
tion approximately satisfies right-rotational invariance, it may
miss dominant paths under extreme compression [3], [17]. To
mitigate this loss, we introduce precoding matrix indicator
(PMI)-assisted beam selection as a low-overhead directional
prior, where the PMI indicates the preferred codebook-based
precoding direction in the channel state information (CSI)
feedback mechanism of the 3rd Generation Partnership Project
(3GPP) 5G New Radio (NR) standard [17]. Simulation results
show that L-STMP obtains concrete reconstruction gains under
this PMI-assisted analog beamforming design, even though the
resulting compression matrix clearly violates the property of
right-rotational invariance.

II. SYSTEMMODEL ANDPROBLEMFORMULATION
A. Uplink Channel and Compressed Observation
We consider a single-user uplink HBF system where a
single-antenna user equipment transmits uplink pilots overN sc
subcarriers, and a base station equipped with anN v ×N h
uniform planar array ofN r receive antenna elements estimates
the full spatial-domain channel from compressed analog obser-
vations obtained through a limited number of RF chains. The
uplink channel from the user to the base station is denoted by
H= [h 1,h 2, . . . ,hNsc]∈C Nr×Nsc ,(1)
whereh k ∈C Nr is the receive-array channel vector on the
k-th subcarrier.
Due to the limited number of RF chains in the HBF receiver,
the base station observes the uplink pilot through a low-
dimensional spatial projection. LetMdenote the number
of compressed spatial measurements per subcarrier, where
M≪N r, and letB∈C M×N r denote a known analog spatial
compression matrix. The matrixBrepresents the effective RF-
domain combining or sampling operation at the receiver. The
received signal is

AWGN matrix with the power of each element beingσ 2. The
pilot symbols are known at the receiver and have constant
modulus with unit power, i.e.,|x k|= 1,∀k. Normalizing the
received signal by right-multiplyingX H removes the pilot
term and yields

whereY∈C M×N sc, andN= eNXH remains i.i.d. complex

B. Result Analysis
Fig. 2 reports the NMSE performance of the proposed L-
STMP receiver and several baselines under random DFT beam
selection with four-fold (M= 64) and eight-fold (M= 32)
spatial compression. The oracle lower bound is obtained by
assuming perfect knowledge of the power angle and delay
profile of the channel, providing a theoretical performance
lower bound [4]. The LMMSE estimator [19] recovers the
channel by a direct linear projection of the compressed ob-
servation. The CNN-based estimator [20] learns a residual
convolutional mapping from the adjoint back-projectionB H Y
to the full channel, representing purely data-driven priors.
DPS [14] performs diffusion posterior sampling with the
same pretrained score prior but requires thousands of reverse
steps, and STMP [16] runs the classical iterative turbo loop
until convergence. Across all evaluated SNR levels, L-STMP
consistently achieves the lowest NMSE among practical meth-
ods, approaching the oracle lower bound. The performance
gap between L-STMP and the STMP baseline widens under
eight-fold compression, indicating that the trainable layer-wise
calibration factors and learned score priors effectively compen-
sate for the mismatch between ideal STMP assumptions and
practical highly compressed observations. LMMSE and CNN
suffer severe degradation as the compression ratio increases,
confirming that model-agnostic or purely data-driven priors

VI. CONCLUSION
This paper proposed a learned score-based STMP (L-
STMP) framework for uplink HBF channel recovery under
spatial compression. By unfolding turbo iterations into a
trainable network, L-STMP replaces the ideal right-rotational
invariance assumption of STMP with learned calibration fac-
tors that adapt to the actual compression structure. Simulation
results showed that L-STMP outperforms linear MMSE, CNN,
DPS, and STMP baselines while requiring onlyL= 5
unfolded layers — a40×inference complexity reduction as
compared to the original STMP. Moreover, L-STMP remains
robust under both random DFT and PMI-assisted DFT beam
selection, whereas STMP degrades under the latter due to vio-
lated right-rotational invariance, confirming that the proposed
approach imposes substantially relaxed requirements on the
compression matrix.
