# Abstract

This paper investigates joint activity detection and channel estimation (JADCE) for grant-free random access in low Earth orbit satellite Internet-of-Things systems without global navigation satellite system (GNSS) assistance. Large differential delays and Doppler shifts complicate the pilot observation model, while fractional offsets weaken sparse channel representations. We propose a multi-frame affine frequency division multiplexing (AFDM) transmission scheme and derive its input--output relationship, which retains the prefix phase for delays beyond the useful symbol interval and separates the Doppler-induced phase evolution across frames, yielding multiple observations of a shared effective channel. A score-based generative prior is then learned from multiantenna AFDM effective-channel samples generated with continuous delays and Doppler shifts, thereby representing off-grid effects and dependence among channel coefficients without a prescribed sparsity pattern. From the posterior factorization, we develop a deep-unfolded message-passing receiver that combines linear estimation with channel and activity denoising. As activity probabilities weight the channel estimates used in subsequent messages, we jointly train the channel denoisers, activity classifiers, and message parameters from received pilots, using score pretraining for initialization and progressive training to adapt the updates to each layer's inputs.
