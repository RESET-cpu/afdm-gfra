## English Abstract

This paper investigates joint activity detection and channel estimation (JADCE) for grant-free random access in a low Earth orbit satellite Internet-of-Things system without global navigation satellite system (GNSS) assistance. The satellite receiver handles the large differential delays and Doppler shifts to reduce the power and computational demands of terminal-side pre-compensation. First, we propose a multi-frame affine frequency division multiplexing (AFDM) transmission scheme and derive its input–output relationship under large differential delays and Doppler shifts, accounting for both integer and fractional offsets. Next, we use score-based generative models to learn a multiantenna AFDM effective-channel prior that accounts for off-grid effects caused by fractional delays and Doppler shifts without imposing a prescribed sparsity pattern. Building on this prior, we develop a deep-unfolded message-passing (MP) receiver with learnable score-based denoisers, activity detectors, and message parameters in each layer. Channel denoising and activity detection are jointly optimized through progressive training and fine-tuning to mitigate the mismatch between the Gaussian denoising model and the inputs encountered during message passing.

## Introduction

Low Earth orbit (LEO) satellites can complement terrestrial networks to provide Internet-of-Things (IoT) services in remote areas. To support access from a large number of IoT devices, grant-free random access (GFRA) allows active devices to transmit without first obtaining a grant, thereby reducing signaling overhead and access latency. However, the receiver has no prior knowledge of which devices are active. Meanwhile, limited pilot resources make it impractical to assign mutually orthogonal pilots to all devices. The receiver must therefore perform joint activity detection and channel estimation (JADCE) from nonorthogonal pilots [1]. In LEO satellite links, this task is further complicated by large propagation delays and Doppler shifts.

To compensate for these effects before uplink transmission, the new radio non-terrestrial network (NR-NTN) framework in Release 17 of the 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) relies on devices equipped with a global navigation satellite system (GNSS) receiver. Specifically, devices use their GNSS positions, satellite ephemeris, and the common timing advance broadcast by the network for delay and Doppler pre-compensation [2]. However, the need for GNSS hardware and frequent pre-compensation increases the cost and energy consumption of IoT devices. To support devices without GNSS receivers or access to GNSS services, Release 19 includes a study of GNSS-independent operation [3]. For resource-constrained devices, handling large differential delays and Doppler shifts at the satellite receiver reduces the need for user-specific pre-compensation. In this case, the differential delay may exceed one symbol duration, while the differential Doppler shift may exceed one subcarrier spacing. Consequently, terrestrial orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM)-based GFRA schemes designed for small timing and frequency offsets and block-fading channels cannot be directly applied to this scenario.

Orthogonal time-frequency space (OTFS) modulation has been proposed to support reliable communication over such doubly selective channels. Its delay–Doppler representation enables the receiver to exploit diversity in both time and frequency [4]. For access schemes that handle large offsets at the satellite receiver, the waveform and transmission design should accommodate delay and Doppler effects while keeping terminal modulation simple. Affine frequency division multiplexing (AFDM) employs a one-dimensional discrete affine Fourier transform (DAFT) with adjustable chirp parameters. Properly chosen parameters allow multipath components to be separated in the DAFT domain and their diversity to be exploited. Meanwhile, AFDM modulation requires only an inverse fast Fourier transform and chirp multiplications, making it suitable for terminals with limited computational resources [5].

Conventional GFRA schemes exploit sparse user activity to formulate JADCE as a compressed sensing problem, solved using approximate message passing or sparse Bayesian learning. In [6], activity and virtual angular-domain channel sparsity are exploited for massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) JADCE. In [7], a sparse prior for LEO AFDM GFRA is refined through a graph neural network whose nodes represent delay–Doppler grid points. However, physical delays and Doppler shifts need not lie on this grid. Fractional offsets cause power leakage across grid coefficients, weakening the assumed channel sparsity. A finer grid increases the dictionary size, whereas estimating continuous offsets introduces additional unknowns coupled with the path gains.

Score-based generative models learn channel distributions directly from samples, enabling MIMO channel estimation without hand-crafted channel priors [8]. In [9], learning the score directly on the AFDM effective channel captures off-grid effects without explicit sparse or grid-aligned assumptions. Multiuser GFRA further requires this learned prior to be combined with activity detection and interference suppression.

In [10], a turbo message-passing (MP) receiver for MIMO-OFDM JADCE combines linear estimation with score-based channel denoising, avoiding online reverse diffusion sampling. However, the denoiser relies on an equivalent Gaussian noise model, which can be inaccurate for finite system dimensions and particular sensing matrices. The resulting mismatch can destabilize the iterations. Damping improves stability by smoothing successive messages, but strong damping reduces the weight of new estimates and can slow convergence [11], increasing the number of linear-estimation and score-network evaluations.

Deep unfolding converts the MP iterations into a trainable network [12]. Layer-specific denoisers and update parameters are learned from received observations to compensate for deviations from the ideal Gaussian message model. In multiuser JADCE, we jointly train the channel denoisers and activity detectors across layers, accounting for the effects of both estimates on subsequent interference suppression. By optimizing the entire sequence of updates, the receiver aims to achieve accurate JADCE with fewer iterations and hence fewer linear-estimation and score-network evaluations.

In this paper, we investigate GNSS-independent GFRA in multiuser MIMO-AFDM satellite IoT systems. We develop a multi-frame AFDM transmission scheme, learn a score-based generative channel prior, and integrate this prior into a deep-unfolded MP receiver. The main contributions are summarized as follows.

1. We propose a multi-frame AFDM transmission scheme and derive its input–output relationship under large differential delays and Doppler shifts, accounting for both integer and fractional offsets. By separating the Doppler-induced phase evolution across frames, the model represents the pilot observations within an access block using a shared effective-channel matrix. Additional pilot frames thus provide more observations without introducing a separate set of channel coefficients for each frame.

1. We use score-based generative models to learn a multiantenna AFDM effective-channel prior for JADCE. Training on channel samples generated with continuous delays and Doppler shifts enables the prior to account for off-grid effects without imposing a prescribed sparsity pattern on the delay–Doppler grid. The learned prior provides channel denoising within the multiuser MP receiver.

1. We develop a deep-unfolded MP receiver that incorporates the learned generative channel prior. The MP iterations are unfolded into a multilayer network with learnable score-based denoisers, activity detectors, and message parameters in each layer. The denoisers and message parameters are jointly trained to compensate for inaccuracies in the Gaussian message approximation. We further develop a progressive training strategy that jointly optimizes channel estimation and activity detection from received AFDM pilots over a finite number of layers.

We also analyze the computational complexity of the complete receiver, including phase and noise estimation.
