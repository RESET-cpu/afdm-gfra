## English Abstract

Grant-free random access in low Earth orbit satellite Internet-of-Things systems must accommodate large differential delays and Doppler shifts when terminals operate without global navigation satellite system assistance. These offsets complicate joint activity detection and channel estimation (JADCE), particularly when fractional delays and Doppler shifts spread each path's energy across multiple delay–Doppler grid coefficients. We propose a multi-frame affine frequency division multiplexing (AFDM) transmission scheme and a deep-unfolded message-passing (MP) receiver for multiuser multiple-input multiple-output access. We derive the input–output relationship with integer and fractional offsets and separate the Doppler-induced phase evolution across frames, allowing the received pilots to share one effective-channel matrix. We incorporate a prior learned from multiantenna AFDM channel samples through denoising score matching into the MP receiver. In each layer, a channel denoiser and an activity classifier produce the conditional channel estimate and activity probability, whose product determines the feedback for subsequent multiuser estimation. Progressive training and joint fine-tuning optimize the denoisers, classifiers, and message parameters using received pilots and their channel and activity labels. This training adapts each layer to its receiver inputs and to the effects of its estimates on subsequent interference suppression.

## Introduction

Low Earth orbit (LEO) satellites can complement terrestrial networks to provide Internet-of-Things (IoT) services in remote areas. To support access from a large number of IoT devices, grant-free random access (GFRA) allows active devices to transmit without first obtaining a grant, thereby reducing signaling overhead and access latency. However, the receiver has no prior knowledge of which devices are active. Meanwhile, limited pilot resources make it impractical to assign mutually orthogonal pilots to all devices. The receiver must therefore perform joint activity detection and channel estimation (JADCE) from nonorthogonal pilots [1]. In LEO satellite links, this task is further complicated by large propagation delays and Doppler shifts.

To compensate for these effects before uplink transmission, the new radio non-terrestrial network (NR-NTN) framework in Release 17 of the 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) relies on devices equipped with a global navigation satellite system (GNSS) receiver. Devices use their GNSS positions, satellite ephemeris, and the common timing advance broadcast by the network for delay and Doppler pre-compensation [2]. GNSS hardware and frequent pre-compensation increase the cost and energy consumption of IoT devices. To support devices without GNSS receivers or access to GNSS services, Release 19 includes a study of GNSS-independent operation [3]. Handling large differential delays and Doppler shifts at the satellite receiver reduces the need for user-specific pre-compensation. The differential delay can then exceed one symbol duration, while the differential Doppler shift can exceed one subcarrier spacing. Terrestrial orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) GFRA schemes designed for small timing and frequency offsets and block-fading channels must therefore be adapted to these conditions.

Orthogonal time-frequency space (OTFS) modulation supports communication over doubly selective channels through a delay–Doppler representation that exploits diversity in both time and frequency [4]. For access schemes that handle large offsets at the satellite receiver, the waveform must also permit simple terminal modulation. Affine frequency division multiplexing (AFDM) uses a one-dimensional discrete affine Fourier transform (DAFT) with adjustable chirp parameters. Properly chosen parameters separate multipath components in the DAFT domain and enable their diversity to be exploited. AFDM modulation requires an inverse fast Fourier transform and chirp multiplications, providing a suitable implementation for terminals with limited computational resources [5].

The channel representation is central to JADCE with AFDM. Conventional GFRA receivers exploit sparse user activity through compressed sensing and use channel structure to improve recovery. For example, the massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) receiver in [6] jointly exploits activity sparsity and virtual angular-domain channel sparsity. In GNSS-independent satellite access, Shen et al. [7] combine multi-frame OTFS transmission with message passing (MP) to jointly recover device activity, channel coefficients, and data symbols, exploiting channel sparsity in the delay–Doppler–angle domain. For LEO AFDM GFRA, the graph neural network in [8] refines a sparse channel prior using nodes associated with delay–Doppler grid points. Physical delays and Doppler shifts, however, are continuous. Fractional offsets spread power across grid coefficients and weaken the assumed sparsity. Increasing the grid resolution enlarges the dictionary, while estimating continuous offsets adds path parameters coupled with the unknown gains. Direct estimation of the AFDM effective channel offers an alternative that incorporates these offsets into the channel to be recovered.

Score-based generative models learn channel distributions from samples and use this information for MIMO channel estimation [9]. For AFDM channel estimation, [10] learns the score of the effective channel and performs posterior inference using the AFDM observation model. This representation captures fractional-delay and fractional-Doppler effects without prescribing sparse, grid-aligned channel support. In multiuser access, the learned channel information must also support activity detection and interference suppression. The turbo MP receiver in [11] integrates score-based channel denoising with linear estimation for MIMO-OFDM JADCE. It uses the learned prior through local denoising updates, avoiding online reverse diffusion sampling. These results motivate combining a learned AFDM effective-channel prior with the joint estimation structure of MP.

Effective use of this prior also depends on the inputs produced during message passing. Score pretraining uses Gaussian-perturbed channels, whereas finite system dimensions and structured sensing matrices can produce denoiser inputs that depart from the equivalent Gaussian model [11]. Damping smooths successive messages, but strong damping can require more iterations to reach a given accuracy [12]. In JADCE, channel and activity errors are coupled through the feedback: the activity probability weights the channel estimate used in subsequent interference suppression, and the channel estimate provides evidence for activity detection. Thus the design problem includes both adapting the denoiser to its received inputs and coordinating the two estimators across successive updates.

Deep unfolding turns an iterative receiver into a trainable sequence of estimation modules [13]. We use this structure to optimize JADCE from received AFDM pilots over a prescribed number of MP layers. Each layer contains a channel denoiser, an activity classifier, and trainable message parameters. Joint training propagates channel and activity losses through their shared feedback: activity supervision adjusts channel denoising, while channel losses at later layers refine the activity estimates used by earlier layers. This provides a direct training mechanism for the interaction between channel recovery, activity decisions, and multiuser interference suppression.

In this paper, we develop a multi-frame AFDM transmission scheme and a deep-unfolded MP receiver for GNSS-independent MIMO-AFDM satellite IoT access. The main contributions are summarized as follows.

1. We derive a multi-frame AFDM input–output model for large differential delays and Doppler shifts, retaining both integer and fractional offsets. Separating the inter-frame Doppler phase evolution places these phase factors in the pilot operator and allows all pilot frames within an access block to share an effective-channel matrix. The resulting formulation combines additional pilot observations without introducing a separate set of channel coefficients for each frame.

1. We develop a channel and activity estimation module based on a learned multiantenna AFDM effective-channel prior. The score-based denoiser estimates each user's channel conditional on being active, and a trainable classifier estimates its activity probability from the denoiser input and channel estimate. Their product is used to form the feedback to the linear estimator, integrating off-grid channel structure and soft activity information into multiuser MP.

1. We unfold the MP receiver into layers with independently trainable denoisers, activity classifiers, and message parameters. A progressive training procedure adds layers and jointly fine-tunes the receiver using channel and activity supervision. By propagating both losses through the inter-layer messages, the training adapts the two estimators to the evolving receiver inputs and their effects on subsequent interference suppression.
