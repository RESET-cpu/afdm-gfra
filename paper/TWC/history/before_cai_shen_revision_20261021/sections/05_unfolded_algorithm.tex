\section{Overall Algorithm}
\label{sec:overall}

\subsection{Message Scheduling}

The conditional receiver takes normalized $\mathbf Y$,
$\mathbf A_{\rm c}$, $\sigma_{\rm c}^2$, and $\rho$.
With pretraining second moment $P_H=\E_{q_{\rm tr}}\|h\|^2/D$, initialize
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf M_1=\mathbf0,\qquad w_1=w_{\rm ref}=\rho P_H.
 \label{eq:twc-initialization}
\end{equation}
A single singular value decomposition of $\mathbf A_{\rm c}$ is
reused. Write $\mathbf A_{\rm c}=\mathbf U\mathbf S\mathbf V^{\mathsf H}$
using its nonzero singular values. Then
\begin{equation}
 \bm\Delta_\ell=\mathbf V
 \diag\left\{\frac{w_\ell s_i}{\sigma_{\rm c}^2+w_\ell s_i^2}\right\}
 \mathbf U^{\mathsf H}(\mathbf Y-\mathbf A_{\rm c}\mathbf M_\ell).
 \label{eq:twc-svd-correction}
\end{equation}

Table~\ref{tab:twc-algorithm} gives the inference dependencies.
The implementation additionally records final-layer feedback diagnostics,
which do not affect the estimates. Depth is fixed across test conditions.
Probabilities remain soft within the receiver, and the final hard
decision does not mask the reported effective channel.
Physical channel estimates are restored by the scale $s$.

\subsection{Numerical Parameters and Information Conditions}

The positive map and damping keep Gaussian message precisions positive.
Their constants are fixed across conditions. Queries outside the
pretraining variance range are recorded rather than silently clipped;
receiver fine-tuning may encounter a different query distribution.
These choices specify a finite computation without claiming convergence
to the working posterior.

For unknown phase and noise, the satellite first uses
Appendix~\ref{app:frontend} to estimate
$\widehat{\bm\beta}$ and $\widehat\sigma^2$, which remain fixed in
subsequent layers. Controlled training supplies the physical operator
and noise variance. All methods must receive the same information
within each comparison. Supplied-parameter results do not establish
performance with estimated parameters.

\subsection{Computational Complexity Analysis}

Let $r=\min(Q,J)$, and let $F_s$ and $F_a$ be the costs of one
user's score network and activity head. A dense thin SVD costs
$O(QJr)$ once per operator. The residual multiplication costs
$O(QJC)$, and applying singular vectors costs $O((Q+J)rC)$.
Trace calculations cost $O(r)$ and elementwise messages $O(JC)$.
Feedback adds another $O(QJC)$ residual multiplication. Thus
conditional inference costs
\begin{equation}
 O\left(QJr+
 L\left[QJC+(Q+J)rC+K(F_s+F_a)\right]\right).
 \label{eq:twc-complexity}
\end{equation}
For $Q\leq J$, linear algebra per layer is bounded by $O(QJC)$.
There is one logical score evaluation and one activity-head evaluation
per user per layer. Batching changes execution time and memory, not
these counts. If the networks contain $P_s$ and $P_a$ parameters,
the independent layers store $L(P_s+P_a+3)$ parameter scalars,
including the unused final feedback scalar.

Equation~\eqref{eq:twc-complexity} excludes offline training and
phase/noise estimation. For the complete receiver, the front-end cost
must be added and measured under the same implementation and hardware
as the baselines. Removing a second-order network alone does not
establish a reduction in total computation or parameter storage.
